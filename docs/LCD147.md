# Waveshare ESP32-C5-LCD-1.47: picture and menu on the LCD

A build of the C5VRX-5 receiver for the
[Waveshare ESP32-C5-LCD-1.47](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-C5-LCD-1.47)
(ESP32-C5FH4, 4 MB flash, no PSRAM, 320 x 172 ST7789 over SPI). The board
has no resistor DAC: the received picture and the on-screen menu are shown
on its LCD. The XIAO DAC build is unchanged.

Status (2026-10-30, one board, PAL VTX on B5 at -38 dBm, bench): the boot
logo, menu and a live black-and-white PAL picture show on the LCD. Over
18 s: 256 pictures, 240 complete (13.3 complete pictures per second), sync
jitter 15 ns, AFC locked. Moving pictures look interlaced (the two fields
of a picture are 20 ms apart). NTSC, weak signals, fades and range are not
checked.

## Build and flash

```sh
idf.py -B build-lcd147 -DSDKCONFIG=sdkconfig.lcd147 "-DSDKCONFIG_DEFAULTS=sdkconfig.defaults;sdkconfig.lcd147.defaults" build
python tools/flash.py PORT --build build-lcd147
```

In PowerShell quote every `-D...` argument (`"-DSDKCONFIG=sdkconfig.lcd147"`):
it splits an unquoted one at the dot. As on the XIAO, do not use
`idf.py flash` on a running receiver: its USB line reset hangs the board
until it is power-cycled.

`sdkconfig.lcd147.defaults` selects `CONFIG_C5VRX_BOARD_WAVESHARE_LCD147`
(menu "C5VRX board", `main/Kconfig.projbuild`), 4 MB flash and
`partitions_4mb.csv` (2 MB application slot, the same NVS and 1.5 MB flight
log). Options there: the SPI clock (`C5VRX_LCD_SPI_MHZ`, 40 MHz as
Waveshare's examples) and a 180-degree turn (`C5VRX_LCD_FLIP`).

## Pipeline

```text
MODEM_DIAG Q4/I4 40 MS/s -> PARLIO RX -> raw 32 KiB ring           (unchanged)
   |-> TX BitScrambler U80P64-R1 -> PARLIO TX, no pins             (unchanged, verified restarts)
   |-> grab.c (CPU, priority 2): FM phase-step discriminator, sync flywheels,
   |      228 x 172 grey (4:3), rows from two fields, at most 70 ms per picture, then 10 ms rest
   '-> lcd.c flush (priority 3): grey or menu rows -> RGB565 4-row strips -> SPI2 DMA -> ST7789
```

- Source format/tap: the same MODEM_DIAG Q4/I4 ring as the DAC build, read
  by the CPU; the CPU never paces IQ. Ring format, descriptors and the
  live TX path (program, LUT, two lookups, DAC20 at TX40) are untouched;
  TX simply drives no GPIO, so the verified restart, OUTPUT and DEMOD
  logic run as on the XIAO (OUTPUT and DEMOD have no effect on the LCD
  picture).
- Demodulation for the LCD is in software: an angle table per Q4/I4 byte
  and the wrapped phase step, nine samples (225 ns) per pixel, [1 2 1]
  smoothed, 51.3 us of each line centred in the active picture, shown 4:3
  in the middle of the panel with black bars. This is a different
  demodulator from P64: picture quality on the LCD says nothing about the
  DAC build and the reverse.
- Auto levels on the LCD: black at the darkest 1 %, white at the brightest
  0.5 %, followed slowly, at most 1.6x.

### What the grabber needs, and what it costs this build

The ring holds 819 us. Any time the grabber does not run for longer than
that, the lines due meanwhile are gone. Four changes, each measured on the
board (console `T`, `LCD` and `LCD_CPU` lines):

1. **The RX descriptor register runs one descriptor ahead of the data.**
   A marker probe (bytes painted into the next two descriptors, polled at
   about 1 us) showed `in_dscr_bf0` naming node N+1 within 3 us of the GDMA
   writing node N's first byte, in all six runs. Rows read up to the
   register's node held the previous lap's samples at their end: black
   streaks (the 0.8-line offset of a ring lap puts that line's sync there).
   `transport_rx_write_offset()` now reports the node before the register's.
   The original grabber's open item ("the descriptor register running
   ahead of the data", wide-search hits at 0-1 sample error) was this.
   The receiver's own control windows choose their source the same way;
   see docs/FINDINGS.md "RX descriptor register one node ahead".
2. **Gain observer period 2 ms instead of 200 us (`GAIN_TICK_US` in
   `receiver.c`).** At 200 us the gain observer, its 64-sample sentinel and
   their timer took ~70 % of the CPU and left the grabber 3.4 % (5.8 ms per
   row); at 1 ms the observer alone took 31 %. The gain core's holds and
   climbs are timed, so it decides every 2 ms instead of every 200 us; its
   reaction to fast fades on this board is not measured.
3. **Control task at priority 1 (below the grabber), grabber rests after
   every picture (15 ms, now 10 ms).** At priority 3 each 50 ms control tick did ~10 ms
   of work in one piece; 158 of 177 grabs stalled 5 ms or more (up to
   90 ms), and since 50 ms is 2.5 PAL fields the same lines were lost every
   time (rows 13 pictures old). Now control runs in the rests: stalls
   above 5 ms 1 in 195 grabs, complete pictures 0 -> 170 of 195. Control
   runs about 5-10 times a second instead of 20, AFC still locks; the
   grabber also rests while the BOOT button is held, because control times
   a press in its own ticks.
4. **AFC window every 300 ms while locked.** Timed on the board with the
   control task at priority 3: a control tick cost ~21 ms of CPU, of which
   the AFC window measurement (`afc2_measure`) 16.5-17 ms, the picture
   measure 1.7 ms, window copy and level 1.3 ms, stationarity 0.9 ms,
   diagnostics 0.3 ms, the AFC decision 0.2 ms. Locked, the AFC only watches
   for slow VTX drift (the lock is sticky), so this build runs that chain
   every `AFC_LOCKED_PERIOD_US` (300 ms, `control.c`); unlocked or settling
   after a retune, every tick as before. The picture measure (`PICTURE`, the
   DAC decoder's output) is not run: nothing displays that output here.
   Control dropped to 4 % of the CPU, the worst stall to 2.3 ms. Doppler is
   not what the AFC follows: at 5.8 GHz even 100 m/s is 1.9 kHz, against
   VTX frequency errors and drift of hundreds of kHz.
5. **A row is shown only when it is intact.** It is extracted into a
   scratch buffer and copied into the shown frame only after checking that
   the oldest sample it read (the one overwritten first) is still in the
   ring; the original checked only the newest. A row missed in one picture
   keeps the previous picture's row; missed longer, it is blended from the
   fresh rows above and below when they are at most 8 rows apart, else kept.

6. **The discriminator's centre is kept within 8 MHz of the tuned
   frequency.** In a movement test (TX carried to and from the antenna,
   -34 to -65 dBm, clipping up to 150 per mille) a window of clipping set
   the centre to about 20 MHz, where the phase steps alias; the original
   re-centring then settled on the alias, and the grabber found no
   horizontal sync for 80 s while the receiver itself showed PAL video.
   A centre beyond 8 MHz now restarts from the tuned frequency (host
   simulation: the old code stays without sync, the new one relocks within
   the same grab). The AFC keeps the swing within a few MHz anyway.

CPU shares now: grabber 58 %, gain observer 20 %, flush 11 %, control 4 %,
sentinel 3 %, calibration 2 %.

The last picture stays on the panel through dropouts; NO VIDEO (with the
channel) replaces it only after 1 s without a single captured row
(`NO_VIDEO_HOLD_US`).

## Menu

The boot animation redraws the rows (clear, then draw) while the display
reads them; a sequence counter (`menu_lcd_seq`, odd during a redraw) makes
the display convert a strip again instead of showing half-drawn rows, which
flickered as horizontal lines.

The same menu, button (BOOT, GPIO28: short = next, hold = select) and
console keys. In this build the menu renders into one byte per logical
pixel (384 x 56) instead of the 98 KiB CVBS raster and its descriptor
chain, which are not built; the display task scales that grid to the
panel (6:5 across, three lines per row). Opening the menu still stops TX
and closing it runs the verified live restart. DAC TEST, OUTPUT and the
PAL/NTSC menu standard have no meaning on the LCD.

## Pins

| Function | GPIO |
|---|---|
| LCD SCLK, MOSI, CS, DC, RST, backlight | 7, 6, 23, 24, 26, 10 |
| IQ loopback (`RF_IQ_GPIO_NUMS`, unchanged) | Q 1/0/25/2, I 27/5/3/4 |
| BOOT button | 28 |
| WS2812 RGB LED (held low) | 8 |

The XIAO DAC pins 23/24/8 are the LCD's CS/DC and the LED here, so the DAC
is never driven. GPIO4 and GPIO5 carry IQ and are also the microSD socket's
CS and MISO: **leave the socket empty** (a card could answer on GPIO5).

## Memory

Internal SRAM is the limit, as on the XIAO without PSRAM. The 39 KB grey
frame (228 x 172) and 21 KB menu rows replace the 98 KB menu raster, and
the 5 KB of strip buffers and the two task stacks replace the menu's
18-22 KB descriptor chain. The first board boot (a 320-wide frame, 8-row
strips, larger stacks) ran out of heap creating the last receiver task;
now the board shows `heap_min=18436` and stack headroom 2204/1844 bytes
(console `T`, `LCD ... stack_free=`).

## Console

`T` adds one `LCD` line (counters since the previous `T`): grabs, complete
frames and frames per second, panel flushes, rows, late and substituted
rows, rows without sync, CPU per row, line period and standard, sync jitter,
carrier offset of the video swing, the auto levels, the last error, the
tasks' free stack and the longest stall per grab (`max_stall_us`, and how
many grabs stalled 1/5/20 ms or more); `LCD_CPU` gives each task's CPU share
over the same span (FreeRTOS run-time statistics, enabled in this build).

`V` dumps the shown grey frame (`LCDFRAME` ... `LCDFRAME_END`, one line per
row: its age in pictures, then the pixels in hex).
